Facts first · transparency always · assumptions never

TENS does not guess.

If TENS has proof, it tells you what the proof shows. If something is only a possibility, it says that. If TENS does not know, it says it does not know. It does not make up an answer just to sound confident.

You set the rules

You decide what TENS is allowed to see, which systems it can connect to, what work it can do on its own, and what still needs your approval. TENS stays inside those rules.

You can see what happened

TENS keeps track of the records it used, the work it did, and the results it found. That gives you a clear trail you can review instead of simply trusting a black-box answer.
